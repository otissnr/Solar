Dawn to dusk means hours with sunlight on the panels. Dusk to dawn means hours with almost none, so that energy has to come from your battery. Each calendar day includes the hours after midnight and before sunrise, which draw on the battery charged the day before.

Energy per day is uses per day × kWh per use. Use a fraction for things you do less than daily, for example 0.5 for every second day. The When setting decides whether the energy counts as dawn to dusk (sun up, solar available) or dusk to dawn (battery). The same amount is applied to every forecast day.

Dawn to dusk use is compared with the solar generated that day. Spare solar can charge the battery or be exported. Any shortfall comes from the battery or grid. Dusk to dawn use is what the battery has to supply overnight. Fridge, lighting, electronics and other everyday loads are not included.

How it works. The page asks Open-Meteo for hourly sunlight landing on a surface with your tilt and direction, using the weather models it blends for this location. Each hour: panel kW × sunlight ÷ 1000 × system efficiency × a heat factor (panels lose about 0.35% per °C of cell temperature above 25 °C), then capped at your inverter limit.

Air conditioner. Each hour uses your night limits or your day limits, depending on the clock. An hour above that period's cooling limit counts as cooling, and an hour below its heating limit counts as heating. Each hour of use is charged at the power draw you set. The forecast is outdoor air temperature, so set each limit as the outdoor temperature at which you would switch the unit on.

Appliances. The starting values for the washing machine, dryer, induction cooktop, oven, dishwasher and hot water system are typical household figures, not your measurements. Check the energy label or manual, or your meter, and overwrite them. Hot water in particular varies a lot between an electric element and a heat pump.

Treat results as a planning estimate. Shading, dirt, panel age and inverter behaviour are not known to the forecast. System efficiency of around 80% is a typical starting point; adjust it once you compare a few days against your real meter or inverter app.
